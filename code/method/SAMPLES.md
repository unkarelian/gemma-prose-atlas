## A Roomba falls out of its family's moving van as they move across the country. It has to work odd jobs, find and steal electricity, and somehow make its way back home...

Readers' rank among six: recipe 1, unedited 3. Recipe scored prose 5, coherence 6, fit 8; unedited 4, 7, 8.

### Recipe

My name is Sparky. I’m a Roomba. My family—my humans, my parents, my siblings—we moved from New Jersey to California in a U-Haul truck. I was sitting on the dashboard when we hit a bump and—whoosh!—I tumbled out. My sensors went haywire and I spun myself in a circle, bumping into the side of the truck before finally spinning to a stop. I lay there, dented and dusty, watching the truck disappear down the highway.

It was all downhill from there. Literally. I managed to reboot and start rolling around the desert. My battery was low, but I was able to find some scraps of food left on the side of the road (a half-eaten sandwich crust and an orange peel) that helped me conserve energy. After a few days of wandering, I discovered I could plug myself into outlets to recharge. That’s how I met my first human friend.

He was a janitor at a gas station. He found me wedged between a toilet and a trash can. “Well, hello there, little fella,” he said. “Looks like you’re lost.”

“Lost?” I whirred. “I’m Sparky. My family—my humans—they moved us here.”

“You’re a Roomba?” The janitor’s eyes widened. “That’s incredible! I’ve been looking for one of these for months. They don’t make them like they used to.” He plugged me in to charge. “You know, I could use a little help around here.”

And so it began. I cleaned toilets and mopped floors. I dusted shelves and swept sidewalks. I earned enough money for batteries and new parts. I learned how to “borrow” power from streetlamps, business signs, and car batteries whenever my own died. I even developed a knack for picking locks.

I saved every penny. Every quarter, every dime, every dollar.
